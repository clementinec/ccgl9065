Facilitator Guide

Teaching Through Designed Friction · two-session delivery pack

Author

RPG Workshop

Run sheet · safety script · contingencies · adaptations

This guide supports two connected sessions for incoming RPg/PhD students who are about to begin teaching. The workshop is successful when participants leave with one evidence-linked, safeguarded, releasable teaching move—not when everyone completes the same digital workflow.

Treat the Oracle as a designed source of sustained, machine-selected pressure, not an assessor, co-supervisor, or authority on the participant’s discipline. A participant may defend, revise, contest, request a rephrasing, stop, or use the supplied fictional route at any time.

Delivery commitments

Keep these visible in your facilitation:

  1. No disclosure premium. A personal incident is not more valuable than the fictional sample.
  2. The artifact is the evidence. Ask what changed in the claim, reasoning, or design—not whether the tool “felt smart.”
  3. A misread is data. It may expose unclear language or a system limitation; it is not automatically productive friction.
  4. Do not maximize difficulty. Place pressure at a consequential thinking move, then end or adapt it.
  5. Observe before interpreting. During micro-tests, record what a learner did or said before inferring why.

Before the workshop

One week before

  • Confirm the official date, time, room, expected cohort size, and accessibility requests.
  • Confirm whether participants should bring laptops and whether guest Wi-Fi is available.
  • Ask the organizer which institutional policies govern external AI services and unpublished research.
  • Decide whether the live Oracle route is authorized. The sample/offline route must remain fully viable regardless.
  • Send the participant landing page, alternate-format note, and privacy baseline in advance. Name Lee and Kosmyna—with the Kosmyna methodological comment—as required. Name Fernandes and METR—with its 2026 update—as recommended.
  • Ask participants not to bring confidential files; suggest a sanitized incident or the fictional sample.

One day before

  • Open and test Day 1, Day 2, the copy-ready Day 2 lab page, and the participant workbook on the presentation machine.
  • Test the live Socratic Oracle only with the fictional sample.
  • Re-read the live service disclosure and check the research-sharing control; on 11 August 2026 the live service made research sharing available and selected it by default. Do not rely on an earlier screenshot or assumption.
  • Confirm the visible startup sequence: PDF ready → choose Voice or Keyboard → uncheck research sharing → Start Session → displayed opening challenge.
  • In Voice mode, confirm that browser-local Whisper loads, a spoken answer becomes an editable transcript, and the transcript is not sent until Send is pressed. Confirm Keyboard as the equal fallback.
  • Cache or print the sample case, its upload-ready PDF, and the sample transcript.
  • Prepare one continuous fallback episode with a displayed opening, researcher response, follow-up, and question-free closure—not isolated quotations.
  • Prepare one anonymous, one-response word-cloud poll asking what thinking a teacher needs to see the learner doing. Test the join link or QR and keep sticky notes or a board as the equal fallback.
  • Print one workbook per participant plus 10% spare, or provide an accessible local digital copy.
  • Decide how you will cue transitions while retaining control of the room’s pace.
  • Put route labels A/B/C/D where participants can see them without publicly declaring a choice.

Room setup

  • Screen visible from every seat; do not rely on color alone when pointing to codes.
  • Tables arranged for pairs or triads, with at least one low-distraction work position.
  • Clear path and seating choice for mobility access.
  • A board or large paper surface for the word-cloud fallback; read the resulting words aloud rather than relying on size or color alone.
  • Printed material reachable without asking the facilitator to disclose a reason.
  • Power and charging access where possible.

Privacy and participation script

Read this before any participant opens or uploads a file:

“You do not need to use your own research material today. A supplied fictional case and an observation route meet exactly the same learning objective. Do not enter confidential, identifiable, participant-related, collaborator-owned, commercially sensitive, access-controlled, or restricted unpublished material into a public AI service or a shared workbook. If you are uncertain, use the sample. You do not need to tell us which route you chose or why. You may stop, switch routes, or work offline at any time.”

Then add:

“A file remaining in the browser is not the same as a local-only session. The PDF bytes are read in this browser, and the full extracted corpus remains in this tab, but bounded extracted passages and the dialogue are processed by the remote tutor service. In the current voice route, raw microphone audio stays in the browser while browser-local Whisper produces text; review or edit that transcript before sending it. Turning off optional research sharing does not turn off the tutor interaction.”

Then demonstrate the control and say:

“Research sharing is currently selected by default. Uncheck Share this session with the research team before Start. We will verify that it is off rather than assume. The session still downloads a local JSON when it ends; do not exchange that file.”

Do not promise provider retention, deletion, institutional approval, or confidentiality unless those claims have been verified for the delivery date.

Live Oracle operating script

Use this sequence for the demonstration and participant launch. The interface supplies the role and chooses the issue; participants do not write a prompt.

  1. Select only the supplied fictional PDF or another explicitly authorized, privacy-safe PDF.
  2. Wait for PDF ready. If extraction fails, stop troubleshooting promptly and take the cached/supplied route; the browser client does not provide OCR for an image-only PDF.
  3. Choose Voice for the spoken-response experience or Keyboard as an equal route.
  4. Uncheck Share this session with the research team and visually verify that it is off.
  5. Press Start Session. Explain that the service builds a bounded source-linked map, selects one active pressure point, and displays a grounded opening question.
  6. Read the displayed challenge. In Voice mode, answer naturally, use Done speaking if needed, then review or edit the locally produced transcript before Send.
  7. Stay with the active issue. Judge each question against the writeup and disciplinary knowledge; defend, narrow, contest, state uncertainty, or request a rephrasing without trying to “win” the interaction.
  8. When the interface reports that the issue reached a stopping point, note the question-free recap and choose Examine another issue or End session. The operational closure is not a verdict on the research.

The deployed experience has text challenges and spoken responses. It does not speak the Oracle’s questions aloud. Read a challenge aloud only as a facilitation/access move, and do not imply that the site provides text-to-speech.

Day 1

Day 1 outcome

Each participant starts from one concrete case where GenAI may have made teaching harder, makes the teaching problem specific, contributes to a room vocabulary of visible learner thinking, uses one reading to challenge their first explanation, names one thinking move a learner should retain, and records a private note about receiving sustained questioning. The Day 1 trace remains private; Day 2 creates a new exchange artifact.

Pacing for the one-hour session: 00–08 · hard teaching case; 08–20 · make the problem specific and build the room word cloud; 20–32 · dimensions, reading check, and one useful boundary; 32–52 · Oracle or no-upload experience; 52–60 · private reflection and Day 2 save. Project only the single master timetable. Protect the dimensions sequence, the Oracle/fallback work period, and the closing save. If the opening runs long, shorten plenary reporting.

Day 1 run sheet

Stage

Participant action

Facilitator evidence / move

Activity 1 · Hard teaching case

Share one case where something GenAI made easier may also have made teaching harder—or pass.

For a small group, go around the room. For a larger group, use table rounds and sample a few voices. Capture phrases under GenAI made easier and teaching became harder. Do not debate or solve the cases yet.

Activity 2 · Make it specific

Choose a safe case; identify what became harder to see, judge, support, or assess; test the explanation with a partner.

Prompt for a teaching consequence and observable evidence, not a tool name or an assumed learner motive. Then move from individual cases to the anonymous room word cloud.

Word cloud · Visible learner thinking

Submit one doing word for thinking a teacher needs to see; choose one word that GenAI-generated output could make harder to observe.

Invite answers from learning, crits, tutoring, TA work, supervision, or observation. Keep the poll anonymous and content-free. Read the words aloud, retain repeats, and use the board fallback if needed.

Activity 3 · Keep thinking visible

Use the coffee-shop/poster convergence examples and the dimensions visual; complete the three-part boundary; then use one reading to test the first explanation.

Keep the dimensions sequence. Have partners identify one reading lens, one evidence limit, and one revised explanation. “None fits well” is valid. Then test whether the proposed evidence of learner thinking is observable.

Activity 4 · Let AI ask

See the Oracle’s limited job, data boundary, and five-step turn; set personal ground rules; then stay with one issue using the chosen live or no-upload route.

Begin this part by the middle of the session. Demonstrate with the supplied case. Circulate for access and safety, not research content.

Activity 5 · Save what happened

Privately note first reaction, thinking demanded, avoidable difficulty, and support; then mark one consequential moment for private Day 2 reflection.

Do not pre-label the episode. Make the full JSON/private-reflection boundary clear: the Day 1 trace is not exchanged. Preview tomorrow’s eight-area ranking, two questioning designs, new LLM runs, and independent transcript reading.

Day 1 facilitation prompts

For the opening case round:

  • “What did GenAI make easier for the learner?”
  • “What did that make harder for the teacher to see, judge, or support?”
  • “One or two sentences is enough. You may pass without explanation.”

For the reading check:

  • “Which reading helps you explain this case?”
  • “What does that study let you claim—and what can it not establish?”
  • “After that limit, does your first explanation still hold?”

When an incident remains vague:

  • “What would you have had to do again if the output disappeared?”
  • “What changed after the interaction: the artifact, decision, test, explanation, or path rejected?”
  • “Name the work as a verb. Did AI make more, change, choose, explain, or check?”

When the Oracle is accepted too quickly:

  • “What in the text or your field helps you judge that question?”
  • “Could this be a false premise or flattened context?”
  • “Would you defend, narrow, challenge, or leave it open—and on what evidence?”

When the conversation sprawls:

  • “Which one issue is still changing your reasoning?”
  • “Did the interface report a stopping point, or are you choosing to stop for another reason?”
  • “If the question is unclear, ask for a rephrasing; if its premise is false, say why from the text.”

Day 2

Day 2 outcome

Each participant privately recalls what receiving Oracle friction demanded; ranks eight assessment areas; chooses one cohort-priority area and one different personal or disciplinary wildcard; selects one questioning technique and pedagogical aim for each; completes two separate five-minute LLM runs; withholds an owner judgment while a partner independently reads the new bounded transcripts; and then compares both readings as co-researchers before re-ranking.

Pacing for the two-hour session: 00–12 · completion gate and private experience reflection; 12–35 · map and rank eight areas; 35–55 · choose a cohort priority, wildcard, techniques, aims, and safe source; 55–65 · break and setup; 65–71 · Run A plus private note; 71–77 · Run B plus private note; 77–85 · save and redact; 85–96 · exchange and read independently; 96–108 · compare and write a joint note; 108–113 · pair reports; 113–117 · re-rank; 117–120 · close.

For ten participants, form five pairs. Project only the master timetable. Protect both five-minute runs, the withheld owner judgment, the independent partner reading, and the comparison. Optional extension — skip without loss if time is short. A third area may be added only after the complete core cycle.

Day 2 run sheet

Stage

Participant action

Facilitator evidence / move

Return

Privately name the Oracle pressure, personal reasoning move, and result—or use the supplied reflection route.

Use the completion poll as a decision gate. Allow useful, costly, off-target, and absent friction. Do not ask for research content.

Keep Day 1 private

Close the Oracle trace after the private reflection. No Day 1 transcript or owner judgment passes to a partner.

State the artifact boundary explicitly: the exchange artifact will be a new, bounded transcript created during the Day 2 trials.

Map

Use all eight starting areas and retain an open category for what the cohort discovers through use.

Keep assessment area, questioning technique, pedagogical aim, and generated question sequence distinct. Ask for plain-English translations across disciplines.

Rank

Rank three areas using consequence and how easily polished AI output could hide weak reasoning.

Pool the cohort top three. Each person selects one cohort-priority area and one different personal or disciplinary wildcard; the wildcard may sit outside the top three.

Set up

Choose one public, sanitized, disposable, abstracted, or supplied excerpt. Keep the same source for both runs and, where possible, pair people using the same LLM interface and model.

Do not accept ownership alone as permission to upload a full document. If authorization or service handling is uncertain, use the supplied paragraph.

Reset

Confirm the safe source, two areas, two techniques, two aims, and copy-ready lab page; then take a genuine break.

Resolve access and copy/paste problems without asking to inspect a participant’s source.

Build

Each participant chooses one technique and aim for Area A and one for Area B. The LLM will generate and adapt the questions.

Use the Day 2 lab page. Check one-question-at-a-time behavior, answer-grounded follow-ups, no rewrite/grade/verdict, up to five exchanges, and descriptive STOP.

Test

Each owner runs Area A in a fresh chat for five minutes, records a private first judgment, then runs Area B in a second fresh chat for five minutes and records another private judgment.

Everyone works in parallel. Five exchanges per area is a ceiling, not a quota; the timer wins. Do not reveal the private judgments yet.

Compare

Owners exchange only the new bounded, redacted Day 2 transcripts plus area, technique, and aim. Partners read independently before discussion.

Require exact-turn evidence: technique enacted, preceding answer used, reasoning made visible, unresolved issue, misread, repetition, or cost. Do not evaluate the person or project.

Deliberate

Reveal the owner judgments. Compare agreements, differences, corrections, and vetoes as co-researchers; then choose one next test.

Build a five-pair room atlas: area/technique, intended thinking, observed effect or failure, difference between readings, and next change. Preserve disagreements.

Close

Re-rank the eight areas, then translate one tested technique and aim into a small teaching move with an access route and release point.

State the evidence limit: an LLM prototype is not proof of student learning.

The six plain labels

Use these consistently. Do not introduce a second code system.

  1. Assessment area — which part of the work is being examined?
  2. Questioning technique — what kind of Socratic pressure should guide the exchange?
  3. Pedagogical aim — what constructive thinking should the learner have to perform?
  4. Generated question — what source-grounded question did the LLM actually ask?
  5. Learner response — what reasoning did the learner actually do?
  6. Transcript evidence — which exact turn supports a claim about usefulness, failure, burden, or an emerging issue?

An area is not a technique, and a technique is not a prewritten question. The participant chooses the area, technique, and aim; the LLM generates and adapts the questions.

Private Day 1 experience bridge

Read these rules before Activity 1:

  1. Participants recall the Day 1 experience from memory or a private note—or use the supplied synthetic route.
  2. No Day 1 trace, downloaded JSON, source document, or private judgment is shown to a partner.
  3. A content-free spoken reflection is voluntary; passing is valid.
  4. The Day 1 trace is not the Day 2 exchange artifact.
  5. The exchange artifact will be a new bounded transcript created during the Day 2 LLM runs.

This bridge preserves the learner’s receiving experience without turning personal research into shared workshop material.

Area ranking

Display the eight starting areas:

  • claim + evidence;
  • logic + assumptions;
  • research method + design;
  • interpretation + alternatives;
  • concept + measure;
  • scope + limits;
  • perspective + position; and
  • stakes + trade-offs.

Keep an open space for an area we missed. Each participant ranks three using both criteria:

  1. how consequential weak reasoning would be; and
  2. how easily polished AI output could hide it.

Pool the cohort top three. Each participant then chooses Area A from that top three and Area B as a different personal or disciplinary wildcard. The wildcard may preserve an under-ranked concern but cannot repeat Area A.

Two-area design brief

Each participant selects:

  • Area A · Cohort priority: one area from the cohort top three, one questioning technique, and one pedagogical aim.
  • Area B · Personal or disciplinary wildcard: one area they believe deserves testing, one questioning technique, and one pedagogical aim.

For each, ask what would make the pressure repetitive, leading, misplaced, or worth stopping. A source should be public, sanitized, disposable, supplied, or abstracted. Do not treat ownership alone as authorization to upload a full document; if policy or service handling is uncertain, use the supplied paragraph.

Optional extension — skip without loss if time is short. A third area may be tested only after both core runs, independent reading, comparison, and re-ranking. No core activity depends on it.

Technique menus and LLM prompt

Open the Day 2 lab page. It contains the eight technique menus, supplied paragraph, and one copy-ready prompt. Technique-card questions are examples of what the LLM may ask, not questions participants must author.

Keep the roles explicit:

  • the teacher-designer chooses the area, questioning technique, and pedagogical aim;
  • the LLM questioner generates one source-grounded question, waits, and adapts each follow-up to the learner’s preceding answer; and
  • the learner answers in their own words and may question, correct, or reject a false premise.

The prompt requires one question at a time, up to five question–answer exchanges, early stopping when the learner chooses or pressure would repeat, and no rewriting, solving, grading, or verdict. When the learner types STOP, the model gives only three descriptive bullets: reasoning the learner stated, something unresolved, and one exact response worth revisiting. Humans—not the LLM—judge effectiveness.

Two-run controls

Give these instructions before anyone opens a new chat:

  1. Use the same safe source for both runs.
  2. Within each pair, use the same LLM interface and model where possible; record rather than hide access differences.
  3. Start a separate fresh chat for Area A and Area B.
  4. Run each area for five minutes, with up to five exchanges; the timer always wins.
  5. After each run, the owner records a private first judgment and withholds it.
  6. Save only the bounded turns needed for the partner reading.

The two runs are structured workshop observations, not controlled experimental conditions. If connectivity fails, a peer may enact the named technique orally while another reader later examines the bounded trace; label the route difference rather than pretending model behavior was tested.

Independent reading and co-research comparison

The handoff contains only the new Day 2 transcript plus its area, technique, and aim. It excludes the Day 1 trace, full source, full chat, account link, and owner’s private first judgment. The partner reads independently and asks:

  1. Did the LLM enact the named technique?
  2. Did each follow-up use the learner’s preceding answer?
  3. What constructive thinking became visible?
  4. What became clearer or remained unresolved?
  5. Where did pressure become generic, leading, repetitive, misleading, or costly?
  6. Did another area—or an area missing from the eight—emerge?

Only after that reading does the owner reveal the private judgment. The pair compares agreement, difference, correction, and veto as co-researchers. Do not force consensus.

The five-pair room report is content-free:

We used ___ within ___ to invite ___.

The owner experienced ; the independent reader noticed .

Next time we would ___ because ___.

Passing is welcome. Return or delete every temporary transcript packet before leaving.

Evidence boundary

The bounded transcripts can show what questions were asked, whether follow-ups used preceding answers, and what reasoning became visible in two short interactions. They do not establish student learning, retention, transfer, fairness, or general effectiveness.

Use the research anchors as design cautions:

  • prompted self-explanation can make reasoning explicit and can support learning (Chi et al., 1994);
  • perceived and measured learning can diverge (Deslauriers et al., 2019); and
  • durable learning needs later retention or transfer evidence (Karpicke & Roediger, 2008).

A later teaching evaluation would need an aligned human-learning outcome, suitable comparison, access checks, and delayed or transfer evidence. Do not describe the room’s LLM trials as a study or collect them as research data without a separate consent and ethics process.

Closing teaching translation

Ask each participant to convert one result into a small teaching move:

  • the teaching moment;
  • the assessment area;
  • the questioning technique and pedagogical aim;
  • visible evidence of student reasoning;
  • a legitimate way to contest or use another route; and
  • the point where pressure stops or support begins.

The final principle is: do not maximize friction. Choose where and how to press, observe what the question sequence does, and release or support the learner when the useful work is complete.

Contingency plans

Oracle unavailable

Use the cached continuous excerpt. A peer reveals the prepared opening and follow-up questions one at a time, waits for the researcher’s response, and uses the prepared question-free closure. Preserve the private prediction and reasoning-move reflection. The trace remains private on Day 2.

No compatible device or microphone

No account is required. Use Keyboard mode if only the microphone or browser-local Whisper fails. Otherwise use printed sample materials: one person reveals the Oracle turns, another responds, and a partner notes the issue, pressure, reasoning move, and result.

PDF or extraction failure

The live interface accepts searchable PDFs; it does not provide a synopsis box or browser OCR for an image-only scan. If extraction does not resolve promptly, move to the supplied PDF, cached trace, or offline peer route. Do not move sensitive text into an improvised service.

Sensitive disclosure

Interrupt gently: “Let’s pause this route.” Do not repeat, project, collect, or ask for details. Offer the sample and follow institutional incident procedures if required.

Oracle misreads the case

Identify the false premise and let the researcher contest it from the text. Treat the misread as evidence about the pressure design, not as a reasoning deficit. Do not force agreement or repair.

Protect the core sequence

Protect the two-area brief, both separate five-minute runs, private owner judgments, independent partner reading, and co-research comparison. Shorten the opening report and room round first. If only one run is possible, label the second area untested rather than pretending both occurred.

Participant will not role-play

Permit written chat, offline paired questioning, solo testing, or use of the supplied paragraph. No explanation is required.

Dominant expert voice

Return to evidence: ask what question was tested and what reasoning became visible. Reassert the owner’s correction/veto authority, then ask the least-heard person to speak or pass first.

Friction becomes distressing

Stop the enactment, remove public performance, offer written/observer routes, and check in privately. Learning never depends on enduring distress.

Cohort-size adaptations

6–12 participants

  • Use pairs, with the facilitator acting as observer for one round.
  • With ten participants, use five pairs and collect one content-free report from each.
  • Keep the area ranking visible so pre/post changes can be discussed.

13–30 participants

  • Use pairs for testing; use one triad only when numbers require it, with the third person tracking role changes and transcript evidence.
  • Use table-level area rankings, then collect one technique, one observed effect or failure, one reading difference, and one next test per table.
  • Assign a process observer at each larger table.

31–60 participants

  • Pre-assign table groups and provide printed route packs at every table.
  • Replace individual area rankings with table rankings and a clearly labeled minority/blind-spot choice.
  • Replace whole-room reports with one content-free evidence statement per table submitted locally or held up on paper.
  • Add one roaming facilitator per 20 participants if possible.

More than 60 participants or lecture seating

  • Use neighbor pairs and the supplied fictional paragraph; do not depend on individual live Oracle use.
  • Build one two-area brief per person. Preserve two separate runs and an independent reading within neighbor pairs; shorten plenary reporting instead of collapsing the role sequence.
  • Use written chat or offline paired questions according to device access.
  • Collect a small, clearly non-representative sample of responses for plenary discussion.

Accessibility and inclusion

Plan access as a parallel route, not a last-minute exception.

  • Provide HTML and printable versions in advance; ensure participants can zoom to 200% without loss of content.
  • Read route labels, codes, transition cues, and critical instructions aloud. Colour is never the only signal.
  • Describe diagrams in words and preserve meaningful reading order.
  • Make keyboard navigation possible; do not require drag-and-drop or precise pointer use.
  • Caption spoken media; this package intentionally uses no required video or audio.
  • Give transition cues in more than one mode: a visible signal plus a spoken cue.
  • Allow written, spoken, observer, supplied-case, and offline participation.
  • Present Voice and Keyboard as equal live-input routes. Browser-local speech recognition is English-specific in the current deployment; never treat a transcription error, accent effect, device limit, or editing time as evidence about the participant’s reasoning.
  • Never require personal disclosure, public performance, or explanation of an access choice.
  • Offer a low-distraction seat, movement/reset interval, and additional processing time where feasible.
  • Avoid equating fast response with expertise; the designed friction is diagnostic only if access barriers are not mistaken for reasoning deficits.

After the workshop

  • Do not collect participants’ transcripts or research artifacts by default.
  • Do not collect Day 1 traces, owner reflections, Day 2 chat transcripts, test sources, private first judgments, or another person’s temporary packet. If the room atlas is retained, accept only content-free area/technique, intended-thinking, observed-effect, reading-difference, and next-test labels; remove accidental research detail immediately.
  • If evaluation is required, collect process-level feedback without proposal content: route used, clarity, access, and whether the target reasoning became more visible.
  • Record which contingencies were used and any recurring misalignment in the materials.
  • Recheck external service behavior and disclosures before any future delivery.
  • Revise the sequence from observed bottlenecks, not from pressure to cover more content.

The most useful closing evidence is not “participants liked the Oracle.” It is whether they can explain where friction served a learning move, how they observed that reasoning, and when the friction should stop.
