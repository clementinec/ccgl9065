3. Prepare to answer the Oracle

The live Socratic Oracle does not give you a prompt box or let you choose the issue. It reads selected parts of a searchable PDF, picks one issue, and displays its first question. Your job is to stay with that issue long enough to notice what thinking the questions demand.

Before Start: the live research-sharing checkbox is currently selected by default. Uncheck Share this session with the research team. This stops the optional research-copy workflow; it does not stop the extracted passages and submitted dialogue from being processed by the tutor service.

What stays in the current browser tab: the raw PDF while it is read, the full extracted text, the session state, and the working transcript.

What leaves the tab for tutoring: selected or retrieved paper passages, the response you send, recent dialogue, and the current session state.

Voice mode: the challenge remains on screen. You answer aloud; browser-local Whisper produces a draft transcript. Review or edit that text, then press Send. The current live voice route keeps raw microphone audio in the browser.

Pre-record before pressing Start

Watch yourself

When challenged, do you tend to rush, defend, over-explain, go blank, or become curious?

Expect

What issue do you think the system may question?

Anchor

What text, evidence, method, or field-specific rule will help you judge the question?

Stop

What would make you ask for simpler wording, reject the premise, or stop?

Live loop

  • I selected only an authorized, privacy-safe PDF—or the supplied fictional PDF.
  • I unchecked research transcript sharing before pressing Start.
  • I read the displayed challenge as a candidate interpretation, not a verdict.
  • In voice mode, I answered aloud, waited for transcription, and reviewed or edited the text before Send.
  • I answered from evidence and stayed with the same issue rather than trying to optimize a prompt.
  • I noted when the interface reported a stopping point and chose whether to examine another issue or end.

If mapping, opening, microphone, or model setup fails, use the cached sample transcript or the offline peer route. Do not improvise with a sensitive document in another service.

Begin as a learner reflecting privately, shift into the teacher-designer role, run two new conversations as a learner, then become a peer examiner and co-researcher. The LLM—not the learner—generates and adapts the Socratic questions from the two techniques and aims you select.

10. Let the LLM generate and adapt the questions

Use the copy-ready prompt on the Day 2 lab page. Keep these roles distinct:

Role Responsibility
Teacher-designer Select the area, questioning technique, and pedagogical aim.
LLM questioner Generate one source-grounded question, wait, then adapt the next question to the learner’s actual answer.
Learner Answer in their own words and question, correct, or reject a false premise.

The prompt must tell the LLM to ask one question at a time, avoid rewriting or grading, continue for up to five question–answer exchanges, and stop earlier when the learner chooses or pressure would merely repeat. When the learner types STOP, the LLM gives only three descriptive bullets: reasoning the learner stated, something unresolved, and one exact response worth revisiting. The LLM does not judge whether the questioning worked.

Pre-run check

  • one cohort-priority area and one different personal or disciplinary wildcard are named;
  • each area has one questioning technique and a plain-language pedagogical aim;
  • the source is public, sanitized, disposable, abstracted, or supplied;
  • the LLM must generate the questions and use each preceding answer;
  • the LLM may not rewrite, solve, grade, or issue a verdict;
  • each area will run in its own fresh five-minute conversation; and
  • the learner can contest a premise or stop at any time.

11. Run two separate five-minute conversations

Use the same safe source and, where possible, the same LLM interface and model for both runs. Start a fresh chat for each area. Each run lasts five minutes and permits up to five question–answer exchanges; five is a ceiling, not a quota. Stop earlier if the aim is reached, the pressure repeats, or you want to contest or leave.

  1. Run Area A for five minutes, then type STOP.
  2. Privately record your first judgment.
  3. Start a new chat and run Area B for five minutes, then type STOP.

12. Exchange the new transcripts; read independently

Prepare one bounded packet for each Day 2 run. Include only:

  • the area, questioning technique, and pedagogical aim;
  • the LLM questions and learner answers needed to show the interaction; and

Do not include the Day 1 Oracle trace, full source, full chat, account link, or owner’s private first judgment. Redact names, sites, sensitive details, and research content the partner does not need. 13. Compare as co-researchers

Only now does the owner reveal the private first judgment. For each run, compare:

  1. Where do the owner and partner readings agree?
  2. What did the partner see that the owner did not?
  3. What does the owner correct, contest, or veto about the partner’s reading?
  4. Did the technique invite the intended thinking, different useful thinking, only more words, or no issue at all?
  5. What should be kept, changed, removed, or tested next?

15. Final check

  • a safe paragraph and a clear context;
  • one cohort-priority area and one different personal or disciplinary wildcard;
  • one questioning technique and pedagogical aim for each area;
  • two separate five-minute conversations, with up to five exchanges per area;
  • LLM-generated first questions and answer-grounded follow-ups;
  • evidence of reasoning, not only fluent wording;
  • the owner’s first judgment recorded privately and withheld during partner reading;
