# 1953: the industry hires a PR firm, not a lab

- **December 1953:** tobacco company heads meet at New York’s Plaza Hotel with the PR firm **Hill &amp; Knowlton**, after studies link smoking to lung cancer.
- **January 1954:** “A Frank Statement to Cigarette Smokers” runs in **448 newspapers**: *“We accept an interest in people’s health as a basic responsibility, paramount to every other consideration.”*
- They fund a research committee whose real job is to keep the question **open** — not to answer it.

<p class="w6-takeaway">The goal was never to prove cigarettes safe. It was to make “we don’t know yet” sound reasonable for as long as possible.</p>

<p class="w6-source">Brandt, <em>The Cigarette Century</em> (2007) · <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Frank_Statement_to_Cigarette_Smokers" target="_blank" rel="noopener">A Frank Statement to Cigarette Smokers</a></p>

# The playbook

- **Fund** friendly research and friendly experts.
- **Amplify** the few dissenters; treat the consensus as one side of a debate.
- **Reframe:** *“the science isn’t settled”* — demand certainty before any action.
- **Attack** the scientists, not their findings.
- **Shift** responsibility to the consumer — *“it’s your personal choice.”*

<p class="w6-takeaway">Same moves, same PR logic, often the same people — first for cigarettes, then for carbon.</p>

<p class="w6-source">Oreskes &amp; Conway, <em>Merchants of Doubt</em> (2010)</p>

# Then the doubt collapsed

- **Documents:** leaked and subpoenaed papers showed the companies knew. Millions are now public.
- **Litigation:** the 1998 **Master Settlement Agreement** with 46 US states — about **US$206 billion** over 25 years.
- **Judgment:** in 2006 a US federal court ruled the companies had deceived the public about smoking for decades (*US v. Philip Morris*).
- **Behaviour:** US adult smoking fell from **42%** (1965) to about **11%** today.

<p class="w6-takeaway">Almost no one now argues smoking is safe. Tobacco doubt is badly weakened — the machine moved on to a bigger product.</p>

<p class="w6-source"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_Master_Settlement_Agreement" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Master Settlement Agreement</a> · <a href="https://www.industrydocuments.ucsf.edu/tobacco/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UCSF Industry Documents</a> · CDC adult smoking data</p>

# 1988: Shell’s confidential report

<div class="w6-kicker">“THE GREENHOUSE EFFECT” · SHELL GREENHOUSE EFFECT WORKING GROUP · MARKED CONFIDENTIAL</div>

- Based on a **1986** internal study; 87 pages.
- Calculated that Shell’s own oil, gas and coal products produced about **4% of global carbon emissions** (1984).
- Warned the changes could be **greater than any in recorded history**, with sea-level rise, shifting rainfall and social disruption.
- *“By the time the global warming becomes detectable it could be too late to take effective countermeasures.”*

<p class="w6-takeaway">This is not a sceptic’s guess. It is the company’s own scientists, about the company’s own products.</p>

<p class="w6-source">Uncovered by Jelmer Mommers, <em>De Correspondent</em> (2017) · <a href="https://www.climatefiles.com/?p=1141" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Climate Files · 1988 Shell confidential report</a></p>

# 1991: Shell even told the public

<div class="w6-kicker">“CLIMATE OF CONCERN” · 28-MINUTE SHELL FILM · MADE FOR SCHOOLS AND UNIVERSITIES</div>

- Warned of more extreme weather, floods, famine and **climate refugees**.
- Called this *“a warning endorsed by a uniquely broad consensus of scientists.”*
- Said action was needed **now**, before the effects were fully certain.

<p class="w6-takeaway">So in 1991 Shell knew, and said so. What did it do next?</p>

<p class="w6-source"><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2017/feb/28/shell-knew-oil-giants-1991-film-warned-climate-change-danger" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Guardian (2017) · Shell knew: oil giant’s 1991 film warned of climate change danger</a></p>

# What they did instead

- **Global Climate Coalition (1989–2002):** an industry lobby — Exxon, Shell’s US arm, BP, the American Petroleum Institute and others — that campaigned against the Kyoto Protocol.
- **1995:** the coalition’s own technical advisers wrote that the greenhouse effect *“is well established and cannot be denied.”* That passage was cut before the document circulated.
- **1998 — API’s communications plan:** *“Victory will be achieved when average citizens ‘understand’ (recognize) uncertainties in climate science.”*

<p class="w6-takeaway">Knowing was not the failure. Choosing to sell doubt instead was.</p>

<p class="w6-source"><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2009/04/24/science/earth/24deny.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">New York Times (2009) · Industry ignored its scientists on climate</a> · <a href="https://www.climatefiles.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Climate Files · API 1998 action plan</a></p>

# Put the scientist on trial

<div class="w7-pair-img">
  <figure><img :src="'../../conversion-assets/data/hockey.jpg'" alt="The hockey stick graph: a thousand years of roughly flat temperatures followed by a sharp twentieth-century rise." /><figcaption>The “hockey stick”, Mann et al. (1998–99)</figcaption></figure>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>1998:</strong> Michael Mann’s 1,000-year reconstruction — flat handle, sharp modern blade. The icon of the 2001 IPCC report.</li>
    <li>Challenged by a mining executive and an economist; neither offered an alternative record.</li>
    <li><strong>2005:</strong> Rep. Joe Barton demands Mann’s funding records, data and computer code. Hearings, op-eds, death threats.</li>
    <li><strong>2006:</strong> the National Academy of Sciences confirms recent warmth is unprecedented in at least 400 years; dozens of later studies agree.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<p class="w6-takeaway">The attack disproved nothing. It bought years.</p>

# The snowball on the Senate floor

- **2003:** Senator James Inhofe calls global warming *“the greatest hoax ever perpetrated on the American people.”*
- **26 February 2015:** he carries a snowball onto the Senate floor — *“do you know what this is? It’s a snowball”* — to dispute that 2014 was the warmest year on record. **It was.**
- At the time he **chaired** the Senate committee responsible for environmental policy.

<p class="w6-takeaway">Weather is not climate. But a snowball is easier to remember than a temperature record — and that is the point.</p>

<p class="w6-source">Inhofe, <em>The Greatest Hoax</em> (2012) · NASA/NOAA global temperature records, 2015</p>

# This is not history: 2025–26

- **July 2025:** the US Department of Energy publishes a climate report written by **five long-standing contrarians** — including Ross McKitrick, who attacked the hockey stick.
- The EPA cites it in proposing to scrap the **2009 endangerment finding**, the legal basis for regulating greenhouse gases from vehicles.
- **February 2026:** the repeal is finalised; it takes effect in April. Lawsuits are under way.

<p class="w6-takeaway">The doubt machine no longer needs to lobby the government. In some places, it now writes the reports.</p>

<p class="w6-source"><a href="https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/03/04/epa-revokes-obama-era-endangerment-finding-on-greenhouse-gas-emissions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ballotpedia News (2026) · EPA revokes endangerment finding</a></p>

# Real uncertainty or manufactured doubt?

<div class="w6-two">
  <div class="w6-card"><h2>Genuine uncertainty</h2><p>Comes with ranges and error bars.</p><p>Published, reviewed, and narrows as data arrive.</p><p>Offers an explanation of its own.</p></div>
  <div class="w6-card"><h2>Manufactured doubt</h2><p>Aimed at the public, not at journals.</p><p>Funded by those who gain from delay.</p><p>Never resolves — and never proposes an alternative.</p></div>
</div>
<p class="w6-takeaway">When someone says “the science isn’t settled,” ask: <em>who profits from your uncertainty?</em></p>

# Will oil go the way of tobacco?

- **Documents:** the internal papers are now public — Shell’s 1988 report, Exxon’s projections, the API plan.
- **Courts:** in 2021 a Dutch court ordered Shell to cut emissions 45% by 2030; the appeal court overturned the target in 2024 but agreed Shell has a duty to reduce emissions. US cities and states are suing oil companies for deception.
- **Hearings:** the 2021 hearing borrowed tobacco’s script on purpose.

<p class="w6-takeaway">Tobacco doubt took about 40 years to break. How long can climate doubt last — and who pays while it does?</p>

<p class="w6-source"><em>Milieudefensie v. Royal Dutch Shell</em>, The Hague District Court (2021) and Court of Appeal (2024)</p>

# Argue your side — without becoming the machine

- **Against the motion?** Argue costs, fairness, retroactive liability, or who else shares responsibility. Do **not** argue the science is unsettled.
- **For the motion?** Use the documents. A dated quote beats an adjective.
- **Everyone:** if you catch a FLICC move — yours or someone else’s — name it.

# The Pre-Mortem

<p class="w6-question">Before you launch, assume it has already <strong>failed catastrophically.</strong></p>
<p class="w6-lead">Then ask: <em>“What went wrong?”</em></p>
<p class="w6-source">Gary Klein, “Performing a Project Premortem”, <em>Harvard Business Review</em> (2007)</p>

---
layout: default
class: w6-slide w6-outline
routeAlias: the-science

# The Science

- This is **prospective hindsight** (Mitchell, Russo &amp; Pennington, 1989).
- Imagining that an outcome has **already happened** raised people’s ability to identify the reasons for it by about **30%**.
- The brain is better at **explaining the past** than predicting the future — so treat the future as past.

# You Just Saw It

- The teams that did well today **anticipated the counterattack.**
- They didn’t ask “what might they say?”
- They assumed: *“We lost. Why?”* — then fixed it before it happened.

<p class="w6-takeaway">The doubt machine runs its own pre-mortems. So should you.</p>
