<span class="eyebrow">TODAY’S DESTINATION</span>

# There is no extra tutorial assignment.

<p class="substatement">Use this time to make the weekly work clearer, stronger and substantially complete.</p>

<div class="outcome-grid">

<span class="section-index">01 · SET UP</span>

# Know what “done” looks like.

<p class="section-note">One compact weekly workflow. No extra tutorial deliverable.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">THE WEEKLY CONTRACT</span>

# One workflow. Two outputs. Four useful parts.

<div class="contract-grid">
  <div><i>01</i><b>Fact</b><span>One specific, verifiable claim with a traceable source.</span></div>
  <div><i>02</i><b>Story</b><span>One real, sourced human example or event that makes the consequence concrete.</span></div>
  <div><i>03</i><b>Spectacle</b><span>One or two sentences connecting the fact, story and stakes.</span></div>
  <div><i>04</i><b>Reflection</b><span>What it means for you, changed in your thinking or now complicates the issue.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="size-strip">
  <div><b>Notion record</b><span>Evidence + reflection · 300–500 words target · 750 maximum</span></div>
  <div><b>Slack share</b><span>Best finding + Notion link · 100–150 words target · 200 maximum</span></div>
  <div><b>Spectacle</b><span>Target 1–2 sentences · maximum 3</span></div>
</div>

<!--
Do not ask students to collect an arbitrary number of facts. One well-chosen fact and one real story are enough if the relationship between them is clear.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">THE REUSABLE RHYTHM</span>

# Two halves, with one protected sprint.

<div class="rhythm-board">
  <div class="rhythm-half">
    <small>FIRST HALF</small>
    <b>Close the current week</b>
    <p>Compare notes, fill the essential gaps and decide which evidence actually earns its place.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rhythm-pivot">
    <div><b>15</b><span>min sprint</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="rhythm-half">
    <small>SECOND HALF</small>
    <b>Prepare the next class</b>
    <p>Find a viable lead, explain it to a partner and choose the next research move.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Adapt the outer timing.</b>
  <span>Protect the two-part logic and the concentrated 15-minute work period.</span>
</div>

<!--

-->

<span class="section-index">02 · CONSOLIDATE</span>

# Make the current entry work.

<p class="section-note">Start with what students already have. Diagnose before searching again.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">SOLO SET-UP · THEN PAIR</span>

# Put your strongest material on the table.

<SessionBrief compact />

<div class="prompt-grid">
  <div><b>1 · Locate</b><span>Mark the strongest fact, story or source already in your notes. Do not begin a fresh search yet.</span></div>
  <div><b>2 · State</b><span>Write the exact claim you think the evidence supports—in one sentence.</span></div>
  <div><b>3 · Complete</b><span>Fill only the missing essentials in the Fact–Story–Spectacle–Reflection chain.</span></div>
  <div><b>4 · Flag</b><span>Mark one place where you remain uncertain. Bring that uncertainty to your partner.</span></div>
</div>

<!--
Allow a short quiet start. If students immediately open a search engine, ask them first to articulate what is missing from the material already collected.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">PAIR EVIDENCE CHECK</span>

# A source is useful only if it survives five questions.

<div class="evidence-test">
  <div><em>01</em><b>Claim</b><span>What exactly are you asking this evidence to prove?</span></div>
  <div><em>02</em><b>Origin</b><span>Can we inspect a credible source—and trace the original evidence where possible?</span></div>
  <div><em>03</em><b>Fit</b><span>Does it match the place, date, scale and population in the claim?</span></div>
  <div><em>04</em><b>Stakes</b><span>Why does this evidence matter to a person, system or decision?</span></div>
  <div><em>05</em><b>Limits</b><span>What remains uncertain, inferred or outside the source’s scope?</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Explain it aloud.</b>
  <span>If the partner cannot restate the evidence and its limits, the argument is not ready.</span>
</div>

<!--
Ask partners to open the source, not just read a citation. Discourage causal language when the evidence only shows correlation or projection.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">THE DECISION GATE</span>

# Every piece of evidence gets one next move.

<div class="decision-grid">
  <div><b>KEEP</b><p>It is credible, specific and already does the job. Tighten the wording and cite it clearly.</p></div>
  <div><b>EXTEND</b><p>It is promising but needs context, a second source, scale or a clearer connection to the stakes.</p></div>
  <div><b>REPLACE</b><p>It is unverifiable, generic, mismatched or less useful than another available piece of evidence.</p></div>
</div>

<div class="source-note">Write the decision beside the material. It guides revision; it is not an additional submission.</div>

<!--
Require one sentence of reasoning. “Replace because it is bad” is not enough; “replace because the article repeats an unsourced estimate from 2019” is actionable.
-->

<span class="section-index">03 · PREPARE</span>

# Find one viable lead.

<p class="section-note">Search with a question. Stop when you have something worth explaining.</p>

---
layout: default
class: timer-slide
---

<span class="eyebrow">CONCENTRATED WORK</span>

# Fifteen-minute research sprint

<p>Find one promising fact, story or source for the next prompt. Record where it came from.</p>

<CountdownTimer :seconds="900" />

<!--
Start the timer after showing the next prompt verbally or in session.json. Circulate and ask students what they are trying to find before suggesting search terms.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">PAIR SYNTHESIS</span>

# Explain the evidence without reading your notes.

<div class="pair-script">
  <div class="speaker">
    <b>Speaker · 90 seconds</b>
    <ul>
      <li>What did you find?</li>
      <li>What might it support?</li>
      <li>Why could it matter?</li>
      <li>What are you unsure about?</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="listener">
    <b>Listener · test the connection</b>
    <ul>
      <li>“The claim I heard was…”</li>
      <li>“The strongest part is…”</li>
      <li>“The missing context or limitation is…”</li>
      <li>“I would keep / extend / replace it because…”</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Then switch.</b>
  <span>Each student records one next action before the pair moves on.</span>
</div>

<!--
The listener is not judging whether they like the position. They are testing whether the evidence supports the proposed claim and whether its significance is intelligible.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">OPTIONAL WEEKLY LENS</span>

# Change the object—not the evidence discipline.

<div class="contract-grid">
  <div><i>01</i><b>Image</b><span>What is framed in, cropped out or made to feel normal?</span></div>
  <div><i>02</i><b>Slogan</b><span>What promise, fear or identity does the phrase compress?</span></div>
  <div><i>03</i><b>Number</b><span>What comparison makes the scale meaningful—and what might distort it?</span></div>
  <div><i>04</i><b>Audience</b><span>Who is expected to act, agree, pay or feel responsible?</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Persuasion is examinable.</b>
  <span>Students may critique the technique while still verifying the underlying claim.</span>
</div>

<!--
Use one lens when it supports the lecture handoff; do not force all four into every session. This is the alternating content layer the teaching team can vary week to week.
-->

<span class="section-index">04 · CLOSE</span>

# Protect the next move.

<p class="section-note">The ordinary work session continues even when optional crits are running.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">FINAL 4–5 WEEKS · OPTIONAL</span>

# The portfolio crit is an added desk—not a new tutorial.

<div class="crit-flow">
  <div><small>1 MIN</small><b>Name the decision</b><span>Student states one question, choice or blockage.</span></div>
  <div><small>2 MIN</small><b>Show unfinished work</b><span>Point to the evidence, sequence or page where the problem lives.</span></div>
  <div><small>5–7 MIN</small><b>Test it aloud</b><span>TA asks viva-style questions; student compares options.</span></div>
  <div><small>2 MIN</small><b>Choose a move</b><span>Student records the next revision they will try.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>10–15 minutes on demand.</b>
  <span>Everyone else keeps working in pairs. The crit creates no extra deliverable.</span>
</div>

<!--
Only show or use this slide during the final four to five weeks. Ask students seeking a crit to arrive with unfinished work and one decision; do not accept “please tell me if everything is good.”
-->

<span class="eyebrow">OPTIONAL CRIT DESK</span>

# Ten-minute critique

<p>Question → evidence → alternatives → student-owned next move.</p>

<CountdownTimer :seconds="600" />

<span class="eyebrow">EXIT CHECK</span>

# Leave with work—not another checklist.

<div class="exit-check">
  <div><i>✓</i><b>Current entry</b><span>The important parts are substantially complete and the weak evidence has a clear next move.</span></div>
  <div><i>✓</i><b>Weekly share</b><span>The strongest portion is ready for Slack, with a link to the fuller Notion entry.</span></div>
  <div><i>✓</i><b>Next-class lead</b><span>One credible source, question or search direction is saved—not just held in memory.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="coordinator-note"><b>If the physical room is full:</b> use the teaching team’s designated weekly Slack thread for the same paired exchange. This is an overflow route, not a separate assignment.</div>
