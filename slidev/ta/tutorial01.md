<span class="eyebrow">WHAT ONE WEEKLY ENTRY HOLDS</span>

# Four useful parts, developed in three stages.

<div class="contract-grid">
  <div><i>01</i><b>Fact</b><span>One specific, verifiable claim with a traceable source and its date or scope.</span></div>
  <div><i>02</i><b>Story</b><span>One real, sourced human example or event that makes the consequence concrete.</span></div>
  <div><i>03</i><b>Spectacle</b><span>Your pitch for your stance — written before you know your in-class role, then refined against it.</span></div>
  <div><i>04</i><b>Reflection</b><span>Two kinds: in your assigned role, and as yourself. They are allowed to disagree.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="size-strip">
  <div><b>Whole weekly entry</b><span>300–500 words target · 750 maximum</span></div>
  <div><b>Spectacle</b><span>1–2 sentences · maximum 3</span></div>
  <div><b>Stages</b><span>Before class + tutorial → during → after</span></div>
</div>

<div class="source-note">The word count covers the finished entry, not each stage separately. Tutorial work belongs to the preparation stage.</div>

<!--
Name the three stages once and move on; students do not need the full assessment logic today. The point that matters now is that the tutorial is preparation time for an entry they will keep developing, not a separate thing to hand in.
-->

<span class="eyebrow">BEFORE THE TIMER STARTS</span>

# Aim for a heading, a question and one sourced fact.

<div class="prompt-grid">
  <div><b>Minimum</b><span>Week 1 heading, your question in one sentence, and one fact with a working source link beside it.</span></div>
  <div><b>If you get further</b><span>Add the story — a real, sourced case — and a first attempt at the spectacle.</span></div>
  <div><b>If you are stuck</b><span>Write the question you cannot answer yet. A named gap is more useful than a filled page.</span></div>
  <div><b>What not to do</b><span>Do not collect ten weak facts. One well-chosen fact and one real story are enough.</span></div>
</div>

<span class="section-index">04 · PAIRS</span>

# Say it out loud.

<p class="section-note">Eight minutes. If your partner cannot restate it, it is not ready.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">PAIR EXCHANGE · 8 MINUTES</span>

# Explain your fact without reading your screen.

<div class="pair-script">
  <div class="speaker">
    <b>Speaker · 90 seconds</b>
    <ul>
      <li>What did you find?</li>
      <li>Where did it come from?</li>
      <li>What might it support?</li>
      <li>What are you unsure about?</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="listener">
    <b>Listener · test it</b>
    <ul>
      <li>“The claim I heard was…”</li>
      <li>“The strongest part is…”</li>
      <li>“The missing context is…”</li>
      <li>“One thing I would check next is…”</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Then switch.</b>
  <span>Each of you writes one next action into your Notion entry before the pair breaks.</span>
</div>

<!--
Pair students who are not already friends where you can. The listener is not judging whether they agree — they are testing whether the evidence supports the claim.

This is the rhythm every later tutorial uses, so it is worth running properly even under time pressure.
-->

<span class="section-index">05 · SHARE</span>

# Anyone want to share anything?

<p class="section-note">Voluntary. Five minutes. Silence is an acceptable answer.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">OPEN FLOOR · 5 MINUTES</span>

# Offer a way in, not a demand.

<div class="prompt-grid">
  <div><b>A finding</b><span>“Did anyone find something that surprised them?” Ask for the source, not just the number.</span></div>
  <div><b>A dead end</b><span>“Did anyone hit something they could not verify?” Naming a dead end saves the room time.</span></div>
  <div><b>A set-up problem</b><span>“Is anyone still stuck on Slack or Notion?” Deal with it now, not by email later.</span></div>
  <div><b>Nothing at all</b><span>If no one volunteers, close early. Do not cold-call in the first tutorial.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="coordinator-note"><b>If a student shares something unverified:</b> respond to the evidence, not the student. Ask where it came from and what it would take to check — that models the whole course in thirty seconds.</div>

<!--
The first tutorial sets the tone for whether students volunteer later. Reward the attempt, correct the evidence gently, and never let a wrong answer land badly in week one.
-->
