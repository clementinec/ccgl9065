<span class="eyebrow">THE ROLE IN ONE LINE</span>

# You are not here to deliver another lecture.

<p class="substatement">You create the conditions in which students can finish work, explain evidence and make their own next decision.</p>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Studio intelligence matters here.</b>
  <span>MArch experience with crits, viva questions and unfinished work is directly useful.</span>
</div>

<!--
This does not make content knowledge irrelevant. It changes how that knowledge is used: as questions, tests and context rather than a substitute performance.
-->

<span class="section-index">01 · PURPOSE</span>

# Support production—not dependency.

<p class="section-note">Students should leave more capable of testing the next claim without us.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">THE COURSE IN ONE SENTENCE</span>

# Evidence-led storytelling about climate response.

<div class="outcome-grid">
  <div><b>Find evidence</b><span>What can be verified, traced and stated at the right scale?</span></div>
  <div><b>Develop an angle</b><span>What is distinctive, consequential or too easily overlooked?</span></div>
  <div><b>Connect the stakes</b><span>Who or what is affected—and why should an audience care?</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Not “win the debate.”</b>
  <span>Students test competing narratives so they can understand why responses succeed, stall or misfire.</span>
</div>

<!--
Debate is one teaching device, not the course’s final purpose. The portable skill is constructing and testing an account from evidence.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">THE WEEKLY OUTPUT</span>

# Small enough to finish. Strong enough to reuse.

<div class="contract-grid">
  <div><i>01</i><b>Fact</b><span>One specific and sourced claim.</span></div>
  <div><i>02</i><b>Story</b><span>One real, sourced human example or event.</span></div>
  <div><i>03</i><b>Spectacle</b><span>One or two sentences connecting evidence and stakes.</span></div>
  <div><i>04</i><b>Reflection</b><span>What it means for you, changed in your thinking or complicates the issue.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="size-strip">
  <div><b>Notion record</b><span>Evidence + reflection · 300–500 target · 750 maximum</span></div>
  <div><b>Slack share</b><span>Best finding + Notion link · 100–150 target · 200 maximum</span></div>
  <div><b>Criteria</b><span>Verifiability · creativity · conciseness</span></div>
</div>

<!--
Students often overproduce because they think volume signals seriousness. Help them select and connect rather than accumulate.
The published rule also applies a one-grade-step penalty when a maximum is exceeded by more than 20%; use it as a boundary, not the session’s central message.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE</span>

# A useful tutorial changes the student’s work state.

<div class="outcome-grid">
  <div><b>Roughly 80% done</b><span>The important parts of the current weekly entry are substantially complete.</span></div>
  <div><b>One evidence decision</b><span>The student can say what to keep, extend or replace—and why.</span></div>
  <div><b>One future lead</b><span>A source, question or next action is saved for the coming class.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="coordinator-note"><b>Tutorial participation:</b> the current student-facing policy allocates 10%, with attendance at a majority of tutorials earning the full 10%. The coordinator will confirm the recording method before term.</div>

<!--
Do not turn “80%” into a grading inspection. It is a confidence-building destination and a defence against unproductive perfectionism.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">ROLE BOUNDARY</span>

# Facilitate the decision. Do not become the author.

<div class="boundary-grid">
  <div><b>Your work</b><span>Ask precise questions, open the source, identify missing context, compare alternatives and protect working time.</span></div>
  <div><b>Student work</b><span>Choose the claim, defend the evidence, decide what changes and write the final language.</span></div>
  <div><b>Useful intervention</b><span>“What does this source actually let you claim?”</span></div>
  <div><b>Unhelpful intervention</b><span>Rewriting the spectacle, designing the portfolio or promising a grade.</span></div>
</div>

<!--
Modelling one revision can be useful. Repeatedly supplying the phrasing or structure transfers ownership away from the student and hides what they do not yet understand.
-->

<span class="section-index">02 · FACILITATE</span>

# Protect the rhythm.

<p class="section-note">Close the current week. Then create a lead for the next one.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">SESSION ARCHITECTURE</span>

# Two halves, one concentrated pivot.

<div class="rhythm-board">
  <div class="rhythm-half">
    <small>FIRST HALF</small>
    <b>Complete + compare</b>
    <p>Students use lecture notes and current work to test what is strongest and fill only essential gaps.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rhythm-pivot">
    <div><b>15</b><span>min sprint</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="rhythm-half">
    <small>SECOND HALF</small>
    <b>Research + explain</b>
    <p>Students find one lead for the next prompt, share it in pairs and choose a next move.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="control-strip">
  <div><b>Open</b><span>Name the destination and prompt.</span></div>
  <div><b>Circulate</b><span>Question before explaining.</span></div>
  <div><b>Close</b><span>Make each next action explicit.</span></div>
</div>

<!--
Adapt the outer timing to the actual timetable. Do not sacrifice the quiet work period every time discussion becomes lively.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">FAST EVIDENCE TRIAGE</span>

# Five checks before a claim earns confidence.

<div class="evidence-test">
  <div><em>01</em><b>Claim</b><span>Is the wording narrower than, equal to or broader than the source?</span></div>
  <div><em>02</em><b>Origin</b><span>Can the student inspect a credible source—and trace the original evidence where possible?</span></div>
  <div><em>03</em><b>Context</b><span>Are place, date, population and units compatible?</span></div>
  <div><em>04</em><b>Causality</b><span>Does the evidence establish cause, association, projection or opinion?</span></div>
  <div><em>05</em><b>Uncertainty</b><span>What qualification would make the statement more honest?</span></div>
</div>

<div class="source-note">The most persuasive sentence is not useful if the source cannot support it.</div>

<!--
If a student arrives with an AI-generated citation, treat the source as unverified until they can open and inspect it. Do not shame the tool use; teach the verification gate.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">A SIMPLE FEEDBACK LADDER</span>

# Match the intervention to the blockage.

<div class="feedback-levels">
  <div><small>90 SECONDS</small><b>Triage</b><span>Name the claim. Open the source. Pick keep, extend or replace.</span></div>
  <div><small>3 MINUTES</small><b>Connection check</b><span>Ask the student to explain how the fact, story and stakes work together.</span></div>
  <div><small>10–15 MINUTES</small><b>Portfolio crit</b><span>Only in the final four to five weeks, on demand, around one student-owned decision.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Escalate only when useful.</b>
  <span>More feedback time does not automatically produce more learning.</span>
</div>

<!--
The shortest intervention should solve most weekly issues. Longer crits are for sequence, argument or editorial decisions that genuinely benefit from deliberation.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">QUESTIONS THAT KEEP OWNERSHIP WITH STUDENTS</span>

# Ask for a decision—not approval.

<div class="prompt-grid">
  <div><b>Evidence</b><span>“What is the strongest sentence this source actually supports?”</span></div>
  <div><b>Significance</b><span>“Why does this matter here, now, to this audience?”</span></div>
  <div><b>Alternatives</b><span>“What changes if the other interpretation is true?”</span></div>
  <div><b>Next move</b><span>“What will you keep, extend or replace after this conversation?”</span></div>
</div>

<div class="coordinator-note">Avoid “Is this good?” as the governing question. Good relative to what claim, source, audience and purpose?</div>

<!--
When students ask for approval, return the work to criteria. This also makes feedback more consistent across TAs.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">CAPACITY</span>

# The paired model can work beyond a 12-person crit group.

<div class="capacity-board">
  <div class="preferred"><strong>Normal tutorial</strong><p>The coordinator sets safe room and staffing capacity. Pair work plus TA circulation can continue beyond a small crit group.</p></div>
  <div class="exception"><strong>Intensive feedback</strong><p>Protect a small queue only for optional 10–15 minute crits. Crit capacity should not dictate the capacity of the entire tutorial.</p></div>
</div>

<div class="control-strip">
  <div><b>Odd number?</b><span>Form one trio; rotate speaker and listener roles.</span></div>
  <div><b>Late arrival?</b><span>Join without restarting the room; receive the destination in one sentence.</span></div>
  <div><b>Dominant partner?</b><span>Use timed turns and require each person’s next action.</span></div>
</div>

<!--
Do not promise a specific enrolment cap in this deck. Room capacity, staffing and safety remain coordinator decisions.
-->

<span class="eyebrow">SLACK OVERFLOW</span>

# Same paired exchange. No parallel assignment.

<div class="handoff-grid">
  <div><b>When it applies</b><span>Physical capacity has been reached and the teaching team directs students to the named weekly Slack location.</span></div>
  <div><b>What students do</b><span>Share evidence with a working partner, question the connection and record keep / extend / replace.</span></div>
  <div><b>What does not change</b><span>The normal Notion and Slack weekly outputs remain the only deliverables.</span></div>
  <div><b>What the TA records</b><span>Follow the coordinator’s confirmed participation protocol; do not invent a local rule.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="coordinator-note"><b>Before term, confirm:</b> the exact channel/thread, how overflow participation is verified and whether it counts only when physical capacity is exceeded.</div>

<span class="section-index">03 · PORTFOLIO</span>

# Deliberate—do not redesign.

<p class="section-note">The student arrives with unfinished work and one decision.</p>

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">FINAL 4–5 WEEKS · OPTIONAL</span>

# A ten-minute crit can have a complete arc.

<div class="crit-flow">
  <div><small>1 MIN</small><b>Name the decision</b><span>What exactly is the student deciding or unable to resolve?</span></div>
  <div><small>2 MIN</small><b>Show the evidence</b><span>Where in the portfolio does the problem become visible?</span></div>
  <div><small>5 MIN</small><b>Test alternatives</b><span>Ask what each option makes clearer, weaker or newly possible.</span></div>
  <div><small>2 MIN</small><b>Commit a move</b><span>Student states and records the next revision to try.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Normal work continues.</b>
  <span>The rest of the room remains in the paired tutorial workflow while crits run on demand.</span>
</div>

<!--
Ten minutes is a clean default inside the 10–15 minute allowance. Use the visible timer in the reusable tutorial deck if a queue forms.
-->

---
layout: default
---

<span class="eyebrow">VIVA-STYLE PROMPTS</span>

# Help the student hear their own argument.

<div class="prompt-grid">
  <div><b>Sequence</b><span>“What must the reader understand before this page can do its work?”</span></div>
  <div><b>Selection</b><span>“If one item disappeared, which loss would damage the argument most?”</span></div>
  <div><b>Consistency</b><span>“Where does the evidence stop supporting the claim you want to make?”</span></div>
  <div><b>Audience</b><span>“What do you want the reader to notice, question or remember here?”</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>End with an action.</b>
  <span>A productive crit changes the next revision—not merely the student’s mood.</span>
</div>

<!--
Do not simulate the final grade. Ask questions that expose structure and choice, then make the student decide what they will change.
-->

<span class="section-index">04 · OPERATIONAL APPENDIX</span>

# Know when to pause and escalate.

<p class="section-note">For incoming TAs: boundaries, escalation and details to settle before term.</p>

---
layout: default
class: dense
---

<span class="eyebrow">TEACHING BOUNDARIES</span>

# Support learning without making promises you cannot keep.

<div class="boundary-grid">
  <div><b>Grades</b><span>Give formative feedback against published criteria. Do not predict marks or negotiate assessment in tutorial.</span></div>
  <div><b>AI + sources</b><span>Treat generated citations and claims as unverified until the original source can be opened and inspected.</span></div>
  <div><b>Wellbeing + accommodations</b><span>Listen without investigating. Follow university and course escalation routes promptly.</span></div>
  <div><b>Disputed claims</b><span>Separate evidence disagreement from conduct concerns; document the issue and involve the coordinator when needed.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="coordinator-note">If a disclosure, conflict or integrity concern exceeds the tutorial role: pause, preserve the student’s dignity, make a factual note and contact the course coordinator.</div>

<!--
The coordinator should replace this general language with current HKU contacts and procedures during the live onboarding. Do not place sensitive case details into public Slack channels.
-->

<span class="eyebrow">THREE COMMON SCENARIOS</span>

# Respond with a gate, not a verdict.

<div class="scenario-grid">
  <div><b>“I found this fact, but no source.”</b><span>Do not polish it. Search for the origin; if it cannot be verified, replace it.</span></div>
  <div><b>“AI gave me this citation.”</b><span>Open it together. If the work, author or quoted result cannot be confirmed, it is not evidence.</span></div>
  <div><b>One partner speaks for both.</b><span>Use timed turns. Ask the quieter student to state the claim and choose their own next action.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="principle-banner">
  <b>Calm specificity wins.</b>
  <span>Name what is known, what is missing and what the student can do next.</span>
</div>

<span class="eyebrow">THE FIVE-MINUTE HANDOFF</span>

# Give every TA the same minimum briefing.

<div class="handoff-grid">
  <div><b>Before</b><span>Read the current weekly task, next prompt and one evidence issue likely to surface.</span></div>
  <div><b>Open</b><span>Name the session destination and working rhythm in under two minutes.</span></div>
  <div><b>During</b><span>Track recurring confusion; question first; keep feedback student-owned.</span></div>
  <div><b>After</b><span>Report policy issues, repeated misconceptions and any concern needing follow-up.</span></div>
</div>
