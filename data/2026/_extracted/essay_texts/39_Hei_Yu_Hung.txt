The Bridge to Zero – Protecting Hong Kong's Self-Employed Drivers in the Age of Oil Crisis 
International oil prices have surged over 55% since the outbreak of US-Iran war.1 The blockade 
of the Strait of Hormuz has disrupted oil supply, particularly diesel, as one-fifth of the world's oil flows 
through this strategic waterway.2 Hong Kong has no oil reserves. Local petrol and diesel prices spiked 
to a record $32 per litre3 within weeks. For self-employed minibus drivers and truck drivers, this was 
not a mere news headline. It was an immediate survival crisis. Some minibus drivers now skip lunch 
to afford the fuel.4 
Context 
Hong Kong's transport sector is the second-largest source of greenhouse gas emissions, 
accounting for nearly 20% of the city's total.5 The government has committed to carbon neutrality by 
2050. Yet oil and coal account for 86% of transport fuel,6 revealing Hong Kong's dependence on 
imported oil with no reserves.7 The current oil crisis makes a permanent exit from fossil fuels necessary.

Electric alternatives cost over one million dollars – far beyond what self-employed drivers can 
afford. The government's three-month fuel subsidy provides instant relief,8 but extending it indefinitely 
would lock Hong Kong into fossil fuel dependency. Withdrawing it too soon would bankrupt thousands 
of drivers. The city is caught between an immediate survival crisis and a long-term climate imperative. 
Solution: “Bridge to Zero” Program 
This is not an either-or situation. I propose a two-phase "Bridge to Zero" program that protects 
self-employed drivers while accelerating the electric transition. 
Phase one focuses on immediate relief. The government has already implemented a three-
month fuel subsidy9 , which has significantly eased pressure — a Tuen Mun resident bus operator 
recently withdrew its fare increase application.10 I contend this is a good move and should be extended 
to 6 months, or until diesel prices return to pre-war levels, to keep the fuel affordable for struggling 
commercial drivers. However, this is not permanent fossil fuel support. It is a time-limited bridge to 
combat the price surge. 
Phase two focuses on conversion to electric vehicles to eradicate fossil fuel dependency. The 
key to accelerating the electric commercial vehicle adoption is accessibility. Given the government has 
fully waived the first registration tax on new electric commercial vehicles,11 I propose launching a 
"one-for-one replacement scheme" on commercial vehicles that provides additional concessions when 
drivers scrap old fossil fuel vehicles for new electric ones. This scheme has proven highly successful

Critics may argue that any fuel subsidy delays the energy transition. I accept this as valid – 
indefinite fuel subsidy does hinder energy transition. However, a transition that bankrupts self-
employed drivers is politically unsustainable and morally indefensible, violating United Nations 
Sustainable Development Goals 1,8, and 10.13 Fuel subsidy is necessary to protect drivers' livelihoods 
during the transition. We cannot sacrifice the vulnerable in energy transition. 
Others may critique that electric commercial vehicles are not ready for Hong Kong’s hilly 
terrain and limited range. I understand that electric commercial vehicles face greater challenges on 
Hong Kong's slopes than private cars. However, electric vehicle technology is already sufficient. The 
expected range of a fully loaded electric truck is as high as 400km,14 which is more than enough for 
Hong Kong, let alone minibuses. The energy transition is already technically feasible. 
Impact and Next Step 
This solution benefits multiple stakeholders. Self-employed drivers gain immediate relief and 
a realistic path to electric ownership. This proposed solution would alleviate the burden on commercial 
drivers, as over 80% have reported mental and physical health impacts from high oil prices.15 The 
climate and general public also benefit, as commercial vehicle electrification reduces roadside NO2

The next step is immediate and achievable. Simply write an email to the Transport and 
Logistics Bureau, “Extend fuel subsidies for self-employed drivers. Launch one-for-one replacement 
scheme for commercial vehicles. Protect the vulnerable in the energy transition.” 
Conclusion 
Hong Kong did not cause the US-Iran war. Self-employed drivers did not cause climate change. 
But both are paying the price. A just transition should not ask the most vulnerable to sacrifice first. The 
government has already shown leadership with the diesel subsidy16. Now, it must complete the bridge: 
extend the fuel subsidy and fund the conversion, forging Hong Kong towards a cleaner future.
