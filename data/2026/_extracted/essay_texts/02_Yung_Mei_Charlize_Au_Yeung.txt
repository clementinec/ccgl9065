 1 From Fast Fashion to Trashion: Reimagining Waste in Hong Kong

In 2023, Hong Kong sent 402 tons of textiles to landfill every day, of that, 245 tons came from 
homes and 157 tons from commercial and industrial sources (MONITORING of SOLID 
WASTE in HONG KONG Waste Statistics for 2023 Environmental Protection Department , 
2024) . What seems like a small daily purchase inevitably becomes part of the city -sized 
urban waste stream, and specifically, how fashion plays a large role in this pollution. 
Fashion is a social language that express identity, status and belonging, but p eople are often 
blind sighted about the damage that fashion leaves behind because its well -packaged and 
disguised by brands to manipulate the public perception. When in reality, the fashion 
industry is a major -league player in polluting the earth. 
The detrimental effect of fashion is a global phenomenon. UNEP reports that fashion and 
textile sector accounts for 2 -8% of global greenhouse gas emissions, consume 215 trillion 
liters of eater, and contributes 9% of microplastics reaching the oceans (United Nations 
Environment Programme, 2025) . In the context of Hong Kong, the government said about 
17,000 tons of waste textiles were recycled in 2022 , yet the overall textile recycling rate was 
still only nearly 11% (LCQ4: Recovery of Waste Textiles , n.d.) . This has been more than just 
a fashion problem, a combination of city, economic, and systemic problems further this 
intensifying issue (Repair and Reuse (R&R) , 2023b). 
My solution for this dilemma is “trashion,” which focuses on reduce, reuse, and the 
recycling of clothes. The solution I have proposed is to treat discarded clothing as raw 
material for a second life. By collecting, repairing and redesigning unwanted pieces into new 
fashionable items, tr ashion will attract the very same consumers to wear their previously 
discarded clothes again. I believe mainstream attempts at promoting sustainable fashion 
fails due to overtold statements that urge consumers to do better. When in reality these 
firms fail to make fast fashion cheap and convenient enough. A real working solution must 
stay affordable and desirable to the public.

 2 Promoting sustainable fashion does not have to start from zero in Hong Kong. The 
government says there are 187 community used clothes recycling banks across all districts. 
The government also claims that reuse is currently the most cost effective way to handle 
used clothes. Systems such as the GREEN@COMMUNITY stations have already been 
organizing upcycling workshops and swapping events to further the progress of sustainable 
fashion (LCQ4: Recovery of Waste Textiles , n.d.) . Instead of utilizing donation bins as the 
primary resort of discarding unwanted clothes, tra shion would help turn these unwanted 
pieces and giving them a second life. Universities, sh opping centers, and community hubs 
would be places for neighborhood trashion stations where clothes are sorted into different 
streams. A torn shirt could be patched or embroidered, and a dress could easily be restyled 
instead of discarded. Each remade item could carry a small tag or an QR code showing 
where it came from and how much waste was avoided. This feature would make pieces from 
trashion unique as it would reveal the pieces past life. 
This idea may sound too unrealistic, but it brings tremendous progress that otherwise would 
not have happened to unrecycled clothes. Reports show extending the lifespan of clothing 
by just nine months could cut its carbon and waste footprints by up to 20% (WRAP, 2024) . 
However, more needs to be done, as research also argues that Hong Kong needs stronger 
infrastructure, and education to promote sustainable fashion (Repair and Reuse (R&R) , 
2023b) . Trashion can be the answer to all, as it creates new partnersh ips between charities, 
designers, and consumers directly. These partnerships would indirectly teach people to see 
old clothes differently, successfully turning climate action into culture. 
From a fashion industry standpoint, trashion is equipped to be largely successful due to the 
impact on its diverse demographic. First, consumers of fast fashion products get access to 
affordable pieces without being told sustainability is only for people w ho can pay more. 
Second, local designers and workers economically benefit from the remanufacturing of 
these clothes. Third, the entirety of Hong Kong benefits as less clothing enters a waste 
system that already faces serious spatial pressure. Most importan tly, trashion redefines the 
value of fast fashion clothes, it replaces the shame of used clothes by giving the new pieces.

 3 Fast fashion thrives because it hides the real costs, but trashion works because it makes 
that cost wearable and bearable. The fast fashion industry in Hong Kong shouldn’t be known 
for the environmental damages it brings. Instead, the industry should be de fined as an 
industry that uniquely positions itself to give clothes a second chance at life. Before buying 
one new fast fashion item, trashion a piece you already own . No matter how small this action 
seems, it’s powerful enough to change habits over time.
