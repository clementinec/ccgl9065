The second, more critical step is Active Debris Removal (ADR). We must 
treat the orbit like a national park that requires maintenance. Missions 
like ClearSpace -1 and Astroscale’s ELSA -M have moved from theory to deployment 
in 2025 -20264, using robotic arms and magnetic docking to capture high -risk objects. 
By targeting the "statistical triggers" —the large, defunct boosters that act as collision 
bombs —we can prevent the Kessler Syndrome before it becomes self -sustaining. 
Furthermore, shif ting to a "Circular Space Economy" where companies are 
incentivized to refuel and repair satellites rather than abandoning them turns orbital 
space from a graveyard into a sustainable industrial zone. 
The payoff of this smart management is the survival of our digital 
infrastructure. Protecting the LEO and Geostationary ecosystems secures a global 
space economy projected to hit $1.8 trillion by 20355. If we fail, the "cloud of death" 
will eventually blind our weather satellites and collapse the GNSS timing that 
synchronizes our global power grids and financial markets. We aren't just saving a 
view; we are defending the nervous system of modern civiliz ation. 
Individuals can accelerate this shift by demanding transparency from the 
private sector. Support the Zero Debris Charter by favoring telecommunication 
providers that commit to debris -neutral launches. Public pressure creates the market 
incentive for companies to invest in expensive cleanup technology. When the cost of 
polluting space exceeds the cost of cleaning it, the indu stry will pivot.
