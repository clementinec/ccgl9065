10 years ago , we could see Tai Mo Shan across Victoria Harbour at the Peak, now we could 
not, not because buildings blocking it, but the particulates and pollutants in the air lowering 
visibility , and the trend is continuing[1]. And we are breathing in this polluted air right now , 
harming our heart and lungs. 
Motor vehicles, especially diesel burning trucks and cars are directly contributing to street -
level pollution, we can feel it when we stand near the end of a bus. Heat from hot engines 
generate nitrogen oxides and the combustion process in engines produces carbon dioxide, 
carbon monoxide and carbon particles, which diesel engines produces higher portion of 
carbon particulates. However, this only contributes to around half of the pollution we see 
today[2]. The other half is regional smog which comes from ele ctricity generation like coal or 
natural gas power plants, although improving, still accounts for 47% of sulphur dioxide in 
Hong Kong’s air[3], a source of pollutants that cannot be missed. 
Here is a solution consisting of two parts. Part one is already in progress, which is for 
transportation to fully go electric, not only for private cars, but for heavy trucks and busses. 
Electric vehicles allow us to bring pollution away from streets, as t hey produce no air 
pollutants, are more energy efficient, and their nature of using electric power builds the 
foundation in setting zero pollutant emissions for transportation. However, 68% of the 
electricity they use are still generated by fossil fuels[4], and that means the pollution is just 
transferred not fixed. This does not mean going electric is useless, this just means we have 
more work to do. 
This brings us to second part which is to stop using fossil fuels in electricity generation. One 
might immediately think about installing solar panels and windmills, they could produce 
enough electricity for a small home, and they are renewable energy that could lower the 
reliance on fossil fuels. However, at least for now solar panels are not efficient in converting 

solar energy enough, and their dependence on conditions like weather means using them to 
power a whole city is not going to work . So here is the proposed solution, to go nuclear. Why 
it works? 1 kg of uranium generates electricity produced by 300 million kg of coal[5], and the 
power plants only produces hot water, and small amounts of manageable nuclear waste that 
decay themselves. Some might fear the danger of nuclear energy after previous accidents 
such as Chernobyl that caused many deaths. From lecture a t week 4, w e see even taking these 
accidents into account, the death caused by nuclear power plants like these disasters per TWh 
is 0.03 , which is much lower than 24.6 of coal mining and their gas pollution , even the 
seemingly harmless solar panels and windmills have 0.05 and 0.04 respectively due to 
maintained and mining rare earth minerals. Here is why nuclear energy is the proposed 
solution for air pollution in Hong Kong, it is statistically safest and can produ ce electricity 
efficiently , meeting out demands without air pollution. 
What could we do to help implementing the solutions ? We could choose electric when buying 
cars, or to choose public transport that uses electricity such as MTR, as well as voting for and 
supporting policy that support other public transport to go electric. Secondly, for successful 
implementation of using nuclear energy for our air, our understanding of this form of energy 
and the fear towards it determines how difficult it is to implement nuclear energy. We could 
educate ourselves about how nuclear energy i s a greener choice, and that speaking from 
statistics it is even safer than renewable energy and of course better than coal. We could stop 
the fear and resistance towards going nuclear. 
Starting from today, choose electric, educate ourselves and others about nuclear energy and 
sustainability , support building nuclear power plants , and with the support and help we might 
save everyone’s heart and lung, and see clear air in Hong Kong after years of pollution.
