The Silent Collapse: The Impact of Warming Oceans on Fishermen 
In downtown St. John’s, fishing nets are pulled, but only 700,000 tons of wild salmon were 
caught , compared to 2.8 million tons of farmed salmon (Seafood Society, 2024). By 2048, 
seafood may vanish, leaving only jellyfish. It seems unbelievable, but this is a fact. Rising 
ocean temperatures is a major driver —over 90% of the past 50 years of warming has been 
absorbed by oceans ( Lindsey & Dahlman , 2025). The livelihood of fishermen now depends 
on sustainable marine practices. 
Unsustainable practices accelerate this crisis . First, o verfishing reduces carbon sequestration , 
while trawling disturbs accumulated carbon stores . 0.34-0.37 Gt of carbon dioxide were 
released annually —equivalent to emissions from over 66 million cars (Atwood et al., 2024). 
Moreover, marine pollutants thickened the greenhouse veil . Rising sea temperatures also 
damage habitats, reducing fish populations . Bleaching -level heat stress scarred 84% of reefs 
globally and destroyed spawning grounds , leaving less polyps and shelter for zooxanthellae —
the base of marine food . Fist population fell, endangering fishermen’s survival worldwide . 
Without imminent actions, global temperatures will surpass the Paris Agreement threshold , 
pushing marine ecosystems toward s collapse . (Hamilton and Feit, 2009, as cited in 
Chandrima Shaw et al., 2023) . 
Sustainable marine practices must be implemented both locally and globally . To begin with , 
the community, notably schools , could hold annual coastal clean -ups and recycle plastics to 
reduce greenhouse effects . Such collective action s are vital, since 79% of plastics end up in 
the environment (Geyer et al., 2017, cited in Shaw et al., 2023), releasing carbon dioxide and 
methane through photo -oxidation. More critically , the tiny microplastics pulverised from the 
estimated 640,000 tonnes of fishing gears lost in the oceans annually are made from synthetic 
plastics and accelerate the emission intensity. (Eurofish International Organisation, 2023) . 
These initiatives foster environmental responsibility from a young age, with research 
stressing that children develop responsibility through activities that connect their actions to 
others (Dementiy & Grogoleva , 2016) . 

 
(Shen et al., 2020) 
Building upon community efforts, government s could also enforce fishing bans to restore 
biological carbon pump . Increasing fish populations elevates carbon sequestrations , since fish 
store carbon and expel the rest as sinking pellets . Indeed , nearly 30% of all carbon 
sequestered by marine organisms is linked to fish (World Maritime University, 
2025) . Complementary measures in corpora te establishing a local r egistration system for 
fishing vessels, limiting new entrants , maintaining sustainable fishing levels , and designating 
fisheries protection areas (Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, 2023) . In 
these areas, restocking periodically could enable growth and reproduction to restore marine 
resources (Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, 2023) . Furthermore , 
indigenous knowledges offer generational guidance for ecosystem restoration , since the y 
include tracking, sustainable fishing and appropriate use of marine resources . Equally 
important , the government should prohibit trawling to reduce damage to the seabed and 
minimize carbon release. Instead, selective gears could be introduced - for instance, excluder 
leadings , which separate codends of varying mesh sizes (Feekings et al., 2023 ). 
Additionally , artificial reef (AR) project s should continue to enhance fisheries resources. 
Monitoring surveys highlighted local ARs host higher abundance and diversity of fish than 
natural habitats, notably commercially valuable breams and snapper s, which use them for 
shelter, feeding and reproduction (Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, 
2023) . Various types of AR may be deployed based on material availability, deployment 
purpose and site conditions (Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, 2023) . 

Most ARs, such as concrete blocks and old vessels , enhance habitat complexity . Others serve 
as biofilters to improve water quality or act as anti-trawling devices to protect nursery and 
spaw ning grounds . Taken t ogether, these measure s safeguard the livelihoods of coastal 
communities. 
Admittedly, p ro-development advocates may contend that sustainable marine practices 
reduce short -term profits for fishers. Nevertheless, by 2100, annual losses from climate 
change for just 16 major U.S. fisheries could range between $278 to 901 million (Moore et 
al., 2020) . This demonstrates that without sustainable marine actions , we cannot guarantee 
human existence, fishermen’s livelihoods, and economic stability in the long run. Therefore , 
fishermen should join the training sessions offered by local government s in transitioning 
toward sustainable marine practices , to adapt more effectively to new measures (Agriculture, 
Fisheries and Conservation Department, 2023) . These sessions usually cover knowledges 
such as the use of selective fishing gears and recreational fishing. For policymakers , 
legislation against unsustainable practices , increase d financial allocation s into artificial reef 
projects and public encourage marine protections are essential . Ultimately , every individual 
should bear the responsibility to advocate for ocean conservations , including recycling 
plastics and supporting products labelled with marine sustainability. 
The invisible , imminent impacts of climate change on vulnerable communities are often 
overlooked until we go off the boundary and are confronted with alarming numbers . The 
silent collapse is reversible, but only if we act now. Saving our oceans is not optional - it is a 
frontline of our fight against climate change and the safeguard of fishermen’ s livelihoods . If 
we all unite in marine sustainable actions , we can restore balance, protect resources , and 
ensure that the future generations inherit sustainable oceans rather than water filled only with 
jellyfish.
