Final Essay

When the thermometer hit 36.9°C at 3 p.m., an old construction worker was still working in construction site under the blazing sun at the brink of heatstroke. When the clock hits 6 p.m., he can finally return to his subdivided flat, hoping for a respite of heat. Lamentably, the ‘cooling’ never came to his rooftop as the entire night stayed at 30.2°C.

Due to outer subsiding air of typhoon Dolphin and El Niño effect, the Hong Kong Observatory recorded 36.8 °C on 9 August 2026, the highest recorded temperature since 1884, and it once reached record-breaking high 39.8°C in Sheung Shui(HKO, 2026). However, the Labour Department still remained the lowest level of heat stress at work warning(HSWW), sparking a huge debate that further unveils heat injustice(Lam, 2026). A study conducted by CarbonCare InnoLab revealed that maximum indoor apparnet temperature in subdivided unit once hit 52.21°C and remained above 50°C at night in summer(CCLI & CSUA, 2026). The poor are living in a steaming oven: children cannot sleep peacefully while the parents can only use a struggling fan to cool him down and desperately waiting for a cool breeze that will never come. What makes he heat injustice worse is that they almost suffer 24/7: 21.4% of the poor take up elementary occupation including construction labour and outdoor cleaner who work under extreme heat and blazing sunlight in daytime(Oxfam Hong Kong, 2024).

Firstly, we must protect them from suffering in daytime by reforming HSWW. Red and black level has never been activated since its introduction(Labour Department, 2023–2026). Although the threshold is lowered, the root cause of the issue is the formula. The current formula uses Hong Kong Heat index(HKHI) which was designed for general public instead of outdoor workers(Poon, 2026). HKHI only gives 5% weight for solar radiation (Hong Kong Observatory, 2023) but most outdoor suffer the most physically due to exposion to blazing sunlight. We should replace HKHI by Wet Bulb Globe Temperature(WBGT) which gives a higher 20% weight for solar radiation. On-site measuremnet shows that HKHI underestimate heat stress. On 11th August, WBGT reached 34.1 degree at Kai Tak while HKHI remained at 30 degree(InmediaHK, 2026). According to Japan WBGT guideline, 34.1°C already triggers the highest level ‘Danger’ but HSWW still remained at Amber under same conditions(Ministry of the Environment, n.d.). Just like ‘truth and manufactured doubt’ taught in lecture, HKHI lacks scientific basis for outdoor workers. We are no longer living in an era when manufactured doubt can mislead public and delay protection. It’s time to use a globally recognized WBGT. What we need is a system thinking and understand the cascade effect rather than simply lowering threshold which leaves the root cause, a mismatched formula, behind.

Moreover, we need a multi-pronged approach to protect the poor from extreme heat in nightime. We’ve learnt urban heat island effect and the builidng accounts for 90% electricty consumption. We need to prioritize green retrofitting, lowering the indoor temperature and improving HVAC system right now, rather than new development. We can provide cooling coatings for sudivided-unit residents as coating can lower indoor temperature by 3°C(PolyU, 2024) without creating financial burden. Also, we should provide air-con electricity-bill subsidies to subdivide-unit residents but not simply giving away an air-con without subsidies since most residents cannot afford the bills and can only use it shortly(The Standard, 2026). This is a pragmatic and multi-pronged transition that were taught in lectures but not a blunt and flawed policy.

With multi-pronged approach, the outdoor workers, the crying children and the residents can finally get a respite. The cycle of suffering from extreme heat 24/7 is finally broken. Heat injustice is something that everyone should concern and it’s time to act now. You can raise awareness of heat injustice by advocating for a reform of HSWW and urging construction company to safeguard workers from heat stroke with cooling facilities. Support the organization concering heat injustice, share the issue with other netizens, keep your air-con temperature above 25°C when possible and care about your elderly neighbours and grandparents during hot nights.

Extreme heat will only get worse but heat injustice can be rectified and broken. Heat injustice can affect everyone across social class. In 20st century, human felt comfortable without air-con but now we are at the brink of heatstroke without air-con. In 2100, is air-con enough for us to survive extreme heat? If we don’t act now, the heat will knock on your doors in 2100. Act now or the heat will devour everyone even with air-con.
