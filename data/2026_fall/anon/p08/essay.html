Causes of the Urban Heat Island Effect

In 2024, Pattie, a 55-year-old cleaner in the New Territories, works outdoors for eight hours every day while suffering from the unbearable heat due to the increased duration of summer in Hong Kong. “Sometimes, when it’s too hot, even the taste of water makes me feel nauseous,” Pattie said. (Lam, 2024). The above case is a vivid illustration of how the hot climate in Hong Kong is threatening people's health with inadequate protections. According to the Hong Kong Observatory, the projection of the 20-year average annual number of hot nights, which is directly linked to the heat island effect, increases from 63 nowadays to 117 in 2100 for intermediate emissions of greenhouse gases, not to mention the high and very high emission scenarios. After analyzing the local conditions, the global data should also be reviewed. If all current pledges and long-term targets are achieved, warming would reach 2.1°C by 2100. (Climate Action Tracker, 2026). These two data provide a full picture of the consequences of climate change in 2100. Noting that the microclimate of Hong Kong is influenced by both the heat island effect and climate change, actions must be carried out to ensure the sustainable livability of our city. With climate change being an inevitable issue on our planet, a few solutions will be proposed to alleviate its impacts in Hong Kong. In terms of governmental policy, more urban greening should be conducted to mitigate the heat island effect. In Hong Kong, parks and green roofs were found to play substantial roles in cooling urban heat, resulting in temperature reductions of 4.9°C and 4.9°C, respectively. (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, 2024). Not only can urban greening enhance the cooling effect through evapotranspiration, but vegetation can also filter out air pollutants to enhance the escape of radiation. As a result, urban greening is an effective measure to create a sustainable city in the future.

However, the feasibility of urban greening highly depends on the structural standards and waterproofing ability of the building. In 2016, a green roof at the top of the Hu Fa Kuang Sports Centre at City U collapsed, with tons of soil and debris rushing into the hall below. With added weight and heavy rainfall being a crux of the incident, this is a walking portrayal of the importance of building structure and waterproofing systems when applying urban greening. Thus, urban greening is inapplicable in old urban areas, but it can be widely practiced during comprehensive redevelopment to mitigate the heat island effect and establish a more comfortable living environment. Apart from urban planning, the healthcare system, which is already under high pressure, must also strengthen its ability to address the increase in the number of heat-related diseases under climate change. The warming climate could increase healthcare demand in the long run. (Lai et al., 2025). With heatstroke, respiratory diseases, and cardiovascular diseases expected to be more common illnesses under global warming, the healthcare system must be equipped with sufficient staff and instruments to deal with these diseases. Last but not least, for education, the syllabus of secondary education should be modified to raise the awareness of teenagers to protect the environment. According to the GEPCC, climate change is taught only as a topic of geography during the F4 to F6 curriculum, which is an elective subject in secondary education in Hong Kong. Consequently, with climate change being an inescapable issue in our society, it should be incorporated as a necessary topic in the curriculum so that all students have a basic understanding of the mechanisms and consequences of climate change. With reference to research done by Pädagogische Hochschule Freiburg University of Education, results revealed a significant medium to large mean effect size for knowledge-related

outcomes for climate change, and a small to medium effect for attitude-related and behaviorrelated outcomes. This study clearly demonstrates the effectiveness of climate change education in shaping the behaviors of our future generation. Considering that long-term mitigation cannot depend only on the government, awareness of climate protection can be entrenched in residents’ minds to alleviate its impacts on our society. To conclude, “I want you to act as if the house is on fire, because it is,” said Greta Thunberg. The evidence provided in the article shows that Hong Kong is already a burning house. In 2100, with the expectation that the consequences of climate change will be intensified, it is necessary for all stakeholders, including the general public and the government, in Hong Kong to cooperate to work out some effective solutions to ensure the long-term sustainable development of our city.
