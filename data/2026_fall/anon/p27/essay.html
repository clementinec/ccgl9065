Every Second, a Garbage Truck of Clothes Is Burned

Fast Fashion and Climate Change: Ending Fast Fashion’s Damage

In Chile’s Atacama Desert, mountains of discarded clothing stretch across the sand. Many pieces still carry price tags. The weather is so dry, so the clothes cannot break down even after a decade. Locals burn these clothes, the toxic smoke from the fire directly harms the local community (Chambers, n.d.).

The same pattern appears worldwide. The fashion industry makes over 100 billion clothes every year (United Natiins Enviroment Programme, 2025). However, many of these clothes are never sold or are thrown away very quickly. This creates a huge amount of waste. In Hong Kong, the problem is visible. the city landfills the equivalent of 17,480 suitcases of textiles daily as people think the clothes are no longer trendy (Texfash, 2025). In addition, the problem is not just about waste the resources, the human cost is just as heavy. On April 24 2013, the Rana Plaza building, which had several garment factories producing clothing for global brands in Bangladesh. Over 1,100 people died during the case as the building collapsed. It brought international attention to the unsafe working environment in the fast fashion industry, which prioritizes low-cost production to meet the high demand for cheap clothing worldwide (Rich, 2023). Therefore, fast fashion damages both our planet and the people who make our clothes.

The solution is we need a new textiles economy system which we will stop making clothes that just follow the trends. Instead of designing clothes to follow the short-lived trends, we design them to keep them in use longer and recycle them after we use them. The Ellen MacArthur Foundation shows that large amounts of non renewable resources are extracted to

produce clothes that are often used for only a short time, after which the materials are mostly sent to landfill or incinerated so this new system is very important that it can help us stop wasting money and natural resources (Ellen MacArthur Foundation, 2017). Today, the fashion industry loses more than 500 billion US dollars every year through the fast fashion trendy. A circular economy approach captures this value and turns waste into useful.

This system connect is three ways. First, changes how we design clothes. Companies design stronger, repairable garments from the start. Second, changes how we use clothes. We can rent clothes, buy second-hand, or keep old clothes instead of buying new ones. Third, build the infrastructure to turn old textiles back into new fibre instead of sending them to landfill. We need new machines and systems to turn old clothes into new fabric. In Hong Kong these steps are practical. The Hong Kong government and local community groups can collaborate together which The government can fund sorting to build recycling factories ; local community groups can teach people how to recycle and repair clothes, more collection bins can be placed in every district and shops can offer take back schemes to customers with discounts These actions can reduce the waste in landfills and create local jobs.

Everyone having benefits for refusing the fast fashion. The environment receives less waste in landfills and less pollution. Workers face fewer dangerous like Rana Plaza as the production slows and the standard rise. Citizens spend less over time by owning fewer and better pieces. Local businesses gain new jobs opportunities in recycling and repair.

Actions can start from today. Before buying something new, ask three simple questions: Do I really need this? Do I already have something similar this? Will I wear this at least 30 times?

If the answer is no, walk away. Also, try to buy second-hand clothes when possible and repair your old clothes instead of throwing them away you can find a clothes recycling bin near your home. Every small action helps change the system.

In conclusion, fast fashion has already left scars in our planet. We must change how we make and use clothes. From the desert in Chile to the landfills in Hong Kong, we can all make a difference.
