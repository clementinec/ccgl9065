The Cloud Has a Ground Floor

The Cloud Has a ground floor Imagine if you could ask an artificial intelligence tool to create a picture of a greener tomorrow. Instantly, a city is drawn up on the screen. It has trees along the streets, with clean waters running through the foundations of buildings. I can still see the creation of this image, but I cannot see what was used to make it. In my work, titled "The Cloud Has a Ground Floor", I added a staircase at the chat window so viewers could follow the process behind the picture. From Hong Kong, I am able to submit a request but I do not know where the processing will occur. So the answer will appear here but the power and cooling will be supplied somewhere else. According to the International Energy Agency (2025), data centers accounted for about 1.5% of global electricity in 2024. This number includes all data centers, including those used for purposes other than artificial intelligence, but it helps me to I know more about the general scale, but I still have the question: what is the resource that the AI service I use depend on? During the discussions of artificial intelligence and climate change in Week 1, I could visualize electricity. Meanwhile, during Week 4, I reflected on where that electricity comes from, as knowing the source will make a difference in the emissions. The life cycle perspective that we discussed in Week 3 helped broadened the understanding of this issue. For instance, the manufacture process of chip could release harmful greenhouse gases (U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, 2026). While replacing AI hardware can also add to electronic waste (Wang et al.,2024) The illustration in my poster indicates another angle of the discussion: some data centers use water for cooling, and generating electricity can also consume water (Shehabi et al., 2024). Thanks to the discussions that we had in Week 10, I can understand further the significance of the blue pipe, power meter, and discarded circuit boards being represented on the same picture. I see the staircase in a more practical sense. AI providers can offer a hyperlink stating "Environmental Information" next to the chat screen. By opening this hyperlink, people can be able to see where the electricity is coming from, the level of greenhouse gases provided by the provider, the quantity of consumed water, and the e-waste. Each one of these points can be addressed separately. For example, a person living near a data center can see directly what is being done with the water in the area. Providers should start with their annual measurements that they can reliably report on for their facilities. The annual report would provide data on the year, location, unit, and what data was captured on the given provider. When a number is reflective of the entire organization in its entirety, then that should be noted. If there is no solid number available from the provider for a specific type of service, then that number should state “not disclosed.” The provider should note how that estimation was made and what the gray areas of uncertainty are. This

would provide an avenue for providers to post pertinent data without claiming that they can measure the effects of all requests placed. The panel can also include the reduction goals beside the actual data. An independent reviewer can verify if the information being presented reflects actual change or just another form of measurement. Users can compare the services when providers measure and report the figure with the same scale. The immediate step that an individual can be taken is to go to the environmental report for the AI service they are using. Select a question such as the source of the electricity. Find out if the answer is only in regards to that specific service or whether the entire company is being referenced. If the information is not clear, then contact the provider for clarity. Even with this visual representation of creating greener cities through AI, I still remain excited about asking AI to draw cleaner cities. This staircase calls upon viewers to stop and think for a moment about the places and resources that made the image possible.
